Merchant acquiring
in the Alfa-Bank mobile app

Together with the team, I designed a mobile merchant acquiring experience for entrepreneurs: from learning about the product and submitting an application to managing merchant locations, equipment, documents, and transactions.

Previously, merchant acquiring was available only in the desktop version. We moved key scenarios into the mobile app, redesigned the landing page and application, and built all sections of the merchant acquiring account.

My role

Senior Product Designer

Team

Product Owner, analyst, 2 iOS developers, 2 Android developers, 2 backend developers, and 2 QA engineers

Result

0 → 17%

Within the first year after launch, 17% of new merchant acquiring sign-ups came through the mobile app

Merchant acquiring application

A key part of the work was reworking the application: breaking it into clear steps, reducing perceived complexity, reorganizing form fields, and rethinking the screen structure.

We conducted UX research on the application to understand how clear the flow was and whether it provided enough information for people to complete it on their own.

This revealed a product challenge: the application had to support independent completion without assuming every user already knew which tariff or terminal they needed.

Structuring the application data

Research showed that the application was difficult not only because of the amount of data. Users needed to understand which details belonged to the company and the merchant location, choose equipment, and assess the tariff. For newcomers, that also meant making decisions without enough experience.

I worked on the flow structure and grouped the information so users could complete the application step by step.

The first step became short and clear: company director, account, payout method, and an email for communication. We can prefill most fields using the client’s main profile.

Equipment

During research, participants lacked information about connection types, device usage requirements, and details about terminals and apps.

In the updated flow, equipment appears in a separate block within merchant location settings. Users can add several devices and review their specifications in detail.

The second step has three levels: a list of merchant locations, location details, and a screen for choosing and setting up equipment.

Tariff

Choosing a tariff was one of the key challenges identified in the research. The solution was to show all tariff terms and let clients estimate their monthly payment based on their own details.

A payment calculator was added to tariff selection, so clients can find a plan that fits their business and compare it with others.
